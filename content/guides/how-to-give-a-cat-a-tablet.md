Start with the easy option: hide the tablet in a soft treat your cat loves. If that fails, place it at the very back of the tongue, hold the mouth closed for a moment, and follow it with a little water or food. Whichever way you do it, stay calm, keep it quick and finish with something your cat enjoys. A pill that turns into a wrestling match gets harder every day.

## Before you start

- **Read the label or ask your vet** whether the tablet can be given with food, and whether it may be split or crushed. Some tablets have a coating that must stay intact, and many taste so bitter that a crushed pill ruins the whole meal.
- **Get everything ready first**: the tablet, a treat, a small syringe of water and a towel. Then go and fetch your cat.
- **Choose a quiet moment**, not straight after a scare or when other pets are around.
- **Wash your hands** before and after, especially with medicines your vet has warned you about.

## Method 1: Hide it in food

1. Give one plain, pill-free treat first so your cat isn't suspicious.
2. Wrap the tablet completely in a small piece of something soft and strong-smelling: a pill pocket treat, a dab of canned food or a piece of cooked chicken. [See pill pockets for cats](amazon:pill pockets for cats).
3. Offer it, then follow straight away with another plain treat.
4. Watch until it's swallowed. Cats are experts at eating around a pill and leaving it on the floor.

Don't mix the tablet into a full bowl of food. If your cat doesn't finish, you won't know how much medicine it got, and it may start refusing that food altogether. Check with your vet before using dairy or human foods as a disguise, since some medicines shouldn't be given with them.

## Method 2: By hand

1. **Hold your cat steady.** Kneel on the floor with your cat facing away from you, its back against your knees, so it can't back away. With a wriggly cat, wrap it snugly in a towel with only the head out (the "burrito").
2. **Tilt the head back.** Hold the top of the head with your non-dominant hand, thumb and finger at the corners of the mouth, and gently tilt the nose up toward the ceiling. The lower jaw often drops open on its own.
3. **Open the mouth.** Use the middle finger of your other hand to press gently down on the small front teeth of the lower jaw.
4. **Drop the tablet in** as far back on the tongue as you can, in the middle. Pills left near the front get spat out.
5. **Close the mouth and let the head level.** Hold it closed gently and stroke the throat or blow lightly on the nose to encourage a swallow. A lick of the nose usually means it's gone down.
6. **Follow with water or food** (see below), then praise and a treat.

## Method 3: With a pill popper

A pill popper (also called a piller) is a plastic plunger that holds the tablet and pushes it to the back of the tongue, so your fingers stay out of the way. Use the same hold as Method 2, place the tip just behind the teeth on one side, slide it toward the back of the mouth and press the plunger gently. Never jab it into the throat. A soft-tipped piller is kinder to practice with. [See pill poppers for cats](amazon:pill popper for cats soft tip).

## Always follow with water

This is the step most people skip. Dry tablets and capsules can stick in a cat's esophagus (the food pipe) instead of reaching the stomach. Veterinary studies have found that pills given "dry" often stay there for minutes, and some medicines, including certain antibiotics, can cause painful inflammation and even narrowing of the esophagus if they linger.

So after any pill given by hand or popper, slowly drip a little water into the side of the mouth with a small oral syringe, about 6 ml (just over a teaspoon), or give a few bites of wet food or a lickable treat. Squirt slowly so your cat can swallow, not choke. [See small oral syringes](amazon:oral syringe for pets).

## If your cat just won't take it

Don't give up on the treatment, and don't skip doses quietly. Call your vet and ask:

- whether the same medicine comes as a **liquid**, a **chewable** or a **flavored treat**
- whether a **compounding pharmacy** can make a flavored version, or a gel for the skin (only some medicines work this way)
- whether a **long-acting injection** or once-a-day option exists
- whether the clinic can show you how to do it, or give doses for you

The same applies to worming and flea tablets. Our [worms guide](/guides/how-to-treat-worms-in-cats/) covers the spot-on options too.

## Never

- Give your cat a tablet meant for people, or one prescribed for another pet. Acetaminophen (Tylenol), ibuprofen and many common medicines are poisonous to cats. If it happens, call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center on (888) 426-4435.
- Push a pill down with your finger deep in the throat, or force a piller in.
- Crush or split a tablet unless your vet or the label says it's safe.
- Change the dose, or stop a course early because your cat seems better, without asking your vet.
- Let a pill session turn into punishment. Stop, stay calm and try later or call the vet.

## Call your vet if

- Your cat drools heavily, gags, retches or foams at the mouth after a pill (bitter taste causes foaming, but ask)
- It seems to have pain swallowing, keeps swallowing, or brings food back up after pilling
- It stops eating or vomits after starting a new medicine
- It spat out or vomited the tablet and you're not sure whether to give another
- You've missed doses or can't get the medicine in at all
- You're bitten or deeply scratched while pilling (cat bites often get infected)

## Questions cat owners ask

### Can I crush my cat's pill and put it in food?
Only if your vet or the label says it's safe. Some tablets are coated to protect the stomach or release slowly, and crushing makes most of them taste bitter.

### My cat foamed at the mouth after a pill. Is that dangerous?
Usually it's a reaction to a bitter taste, and it settles in a few minutes. If it doesn't, or your cat seems unwell, call your vet.

### What if my cat spits the pill out?
If it's whole and dry, try once more straight away. If it's partly dissolved or you're unsure how much went down, call your vet before giving another.
