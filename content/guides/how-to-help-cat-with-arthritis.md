Arthritis is very common in older cats, but it's easy to miss. Cats are experts at hiding pain, and they rarely limp. Instead, they quietly stop jumping up to their favorite spots, sleep more and groom less, and many owners put it down to "just getting old". Studies using X-rays suggest that most cats over 12 have some degree of joint disease. Arthritis can't be cured, but it is very treatable, and the right pain relief plus a few changes at home can make an older cat noticeably happier and more active.

## Signs of arthritis in cats

The signs are usually changes in behavior rather than an obvious limp:

- **Jumping less**, jumping onto lower surfaces, or hesitating before a jump
- **Climbing stairs slowly** or one step at a time
- **Stiffness**, especially after sleeping
- **Grooming less**, leading to a scruffy or matted coat on the back and hips
- **Overgrown claws**, because your cat scratches less
- **Accidents outside the litter box**, often because stepping into the box hurts
- **Sleeping more**, playing less or being less interested in people
- **Irritability** or flinching when touched along the back or hips
- **Hiding** more than usual

Try watching your cat for a week and noting anything it no longer does. Short videos of your cat walking, jumping or using stairs can help your vet a lot.

## What causes it

Arthritis (also called degenerative joint disease) happens when the cartilage that cushions the joints wears down, causing pain, stiffness and inflammation. The hips, elbows, knees and spine are often affected. Risk factors include:

- **Age**, by far the biggest factor
- **Being overweight**, which puts more strain on the joints
- **Old injuries**, such as fractures or joint damage
- **Joint problems you're born with**, such as hip dysplasia, which is more common in some breeds, including Maine Coons and Persians

## How vets treat arthritis

There's no cure, but there's a lot your vet can do to reduce pain and keep your cat moving. Your vet will examine your cat's joints and may suggest X-rays and blood tests, especially before starting long-term medicine.

- **Pain relief.** Several medicines are licensed for cats with arthritis, including anti-inflammatory medicines and a monthly injection that targets nerve growth factor, a key driver of arthritis pain. Your vet will choose what suits your cat, especially if it has kidney or other health problems, and give it exactly as your vet prescribes.
- **Weight loss.** For overweight cats, slow, steady weight loss with your vet's guidance is one of the most effective ways to ease joint pain.
- **Joint diets and supplements.** Some prescription diets and omega-3 supplements may help. Ask your vet before starting any supplement.
- **Physical therapy.** Some clinics offer gentle exercise programs, massage or laser therapy for cats.

Many owners are surprised by how much more active and affectionate their cat becomes once its pain is controlled.

## How to help at home

1. **Make high places easy to reach.** Add steps, ramps or a sturdy chair so your cat can still reach the bed, sofa or window ([see pet stairs](amazon:pet stairs for cats)). A lower cat tree with steps can also help (see our guide to [the best cat trees](/guides/best-cat-trees-for-indoor-cats/)).
2. **Use a low-entry litter box.** Choose a large box with a low side your cat can step over, and put one on every floor (see our guide to [choosing a litter box](/guides/how-to-choose-a-litter-box/)).
3. **Give a warm, soft bed.** Arthritic joints feel better when warm. A thick orthopedic or heated pet bed in a draft-free spot is ideal (see our guide to [the best cat beds](/guides/best-cat-beds/)).
4. **Keep food and water easy to reach.** Put bowls at floor level or on a low step, not on a counter your cat has to jump onto.
5. **Help with grooming.** Gently brush the back and hips, which your cat may no longer reach. Use a soft brush and short sessions.
6. **Trim the claws.** Arthritic cats scratch less, so claws can overgrow and curl into the paw pads. Ask your vet to show you how, or have them trimmed regularly.
7. **Keep your cat gently active.** Short play sessions with a wand toy held low to the ground keep muscles strong without high jumps.
8. **Help your cat stay slim** with measured meals and fewer treats.

## Never

- **Give human painkillers.** **Acetaminophen (Tylenol) is deadly to cats**, even in small amounts. Ibuprofen, naproxen and aspirin can cause stomach ulcers and kidney failure.
- **Give dog arthritis medicine** or another pet's prescription. Doses and ingredients that are safe for dogs can be dangerous for cats.
- **Start supplements or "natural" remedies without asking your vet.** Some interact with medicines, and some essential oils and herbs are toxic to cats.
- **Assume it's just old age.** Slowing down is often pain, and pain can be treated.

## See a vet if

- Your cat has **stopped jumping**, is stiff, or shows any of the signs above
- Your cat is **limping**, or a joint looks swollen or hot (this can be an injury or infection, not arthritis)
- Your cat **cries out** when picked up or touched
- There are **litter box accidents**, which can also be a sign of kidney, bladder or bowel problems
- Your cat on arthritis medicine **vomits, has diarrhea, stops eating or drinks much more**. Stop the medicine and call your vet.

Senior cats should see the vet at least once or twice a year, so changes can be picked up early.

## Your questions answered

### How can I tell if my cat has arthritis?

Look for small changes: jumping less or lower, stiffness after rest, a scruffy coat on the back, overgrown claws or litter box accidents. Your vet can examine the joints and may suggest X-rays.

### Can young cats get arthritis?

Yes, though it's less common. Young cats can develop arthritis after an injury, or because of a joint problem they were born with, such as hip dysplasia.

### What can I give my cat for arthritis pain?

Only medicine your vet prescribes. Several safe, effective options are licensed for cats. Never give human or dog painkillers, which can be deadly to cats.

### Do joint supplements work for cats?

Some, such as omega-3 fatty acids, may help a little alongside other treatment. The evidence is mixed, so ask your vet which, if any, suit your cat.

### How long can a cat live with arthritis?

Arthritis itself doesn't usually shorten a cat's life. With good pain control, weight management and a few changes at home, most arthritic cats live comfortably for years.

## The short version

Arthritis is very common in older cats, but the signs are subtle: jumping less, stiffness, grooming less and litter box accidents. See your vet for safe pain relief, never give human painkillers, and make life easier with steps, a low-entry litter box and a warm bed.
