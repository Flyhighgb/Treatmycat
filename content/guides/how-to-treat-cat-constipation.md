Most cats poop at least once a day. A cat that goes **more than 48 hours without pooping**, or passes small, hard, dry stools, may be constipated. It's especially common in older cats.

## First: is it really constipation?

**A cat straining in the litter tray and producing little or no urine is an emergency**, especially a male cat. A urinary blockage looks very similar to constipation and can be fatal within a day or two. If you can't tell whether your cat is trying to poop or pee, **call your vet straight away**.

## Signs of constipation

- Straining, or spending a long time in the litter tray
- Small, hard, dry stools, or none at all
- Crying or discomfort when trying to go
- Stools left outside the tray (it may be associating the tray with pain)
- Less appetite, vomiting or lethargy in more severe cases

## Common causes

- **Not drinking enough.** Cats on dry food only are at higher risk.
- Swallowed hair
- Not enough exercise, or being overweight
- Pain, such as arthritis making it hard to squat or climb into the tray
- A dirty tray, or a tray in a stressful spot, so the cat holds on
- Underlying illness, like kidney disease, diabetes or megacolon

## Gentle ways to help at home

For a mild case in an otherwise well cat:

1. **More water.** Switch some or all meals to wet food, add a little water to meals, and try a water fountain. This makes the biggest difference.
2. **More fibre.** A high-fibre or gut-health food can help. Make changes gradually.
3. **More movement.** Play sessions with wand toys get the gut moving too.
4. **Make the litter tray easy.** Keep it scooped, use a tray with a low entrance for older or arthritic cats, and put it somewhere quiet.
5. **Brush regularly** to cut down on swallowed hair.

## Don't

- Give human laxatives, stool softeners or enemas. Some human enemas (phosphate types) are toxic to cats.
- Give oil by mouth. Cats can inhale it into their lungs.

## See a vet if

- No stool for **48 to 72 hours**
- Your cat is vomiting, not eating, or seems unwell
- There's blood, or obvious pain
- Constipation keeps coming back

Your vet can give safe laxatives, an enema under supervision, or fluids, and check for underlying causes like kidney disease or arthritis.

## The short version

Most constipation comes down to not enough water. Wet food is usually the fix. But straining with nothing coming out could be a urinary blockage, which is an emergency.
