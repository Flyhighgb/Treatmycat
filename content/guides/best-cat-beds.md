Cats sleep for around 12 to 16 hours a day, so where they sleep matters. Many expensive beds end up ignored while the cat naps in a cardboard box. The secret is matching the bed to how your cat likes to sleep and putting it in the right place. The quick answer: most cats love a **warm, soft bed with raised sides** (a bolster or donut bed), placed somewhere **quiet, warm and slightly raised**, like near a sunny window.

## How cats like to sleep

Watch your cat for a few days before you buy.

- **Curled up in a ball?** It will probably like a round donut or bolster bed with sides to lean against.
- **Stretched out flat?** Choose a larger, flat or cushioned mat-style bed.
- **Hides under blankets or in boxes?** A cave or hooded bed gives it the enclosed, safe feeling it wants.
- **Sleeps up high?** Try a window perch, a bed on a cat tree, or a hammock.
- **Always in the sunniest or warmest spot?** A self-warming or heated bed may become its favorite.

## What to look for

**Warmth.** Cats like it warmer than we do. Thick, fleecy or faux-fur fabrics, and raised sides that block drafts, help a lot.

**The right size.** A bed that's too big doesn't feel snug. Your cat should fit with a little room to spare when curled up or stretched out.

**Washable.** Look for a machine-washable cover or a bed that can go in the washing machine whole. Beds collect hair, dander and sometimes fleas.

**Support for older cats.** Senior and arthritic cats benefit from thicker, orthopedic foam and a low, easy entry.

**Safe materials.** Avoid beds with loose threads, dangling cords or small parts your cat could chew off and swallow.

## Where to put the bed

Location matters as much as the bed itself.

1. **Somewhere warm**, such as a sunny spot or near (not on) a radiator, and out of drafts.
2. **Somewhere quiet**, away from busy doorways, loud appliances and the dog's bed.
3. **Slightly raised**, if your cat likes to watch the room. A bed on a chair, shelf or cat tree often gets more use than one on the floor.
4. **Near a window**, for cats that like to watch birds.
5. **Away from the litter box and food bowls.**

Many cats like a few different sleeping spots and rotate between them through the day. In multi-cat homes, provide more beds than you have cats, in different rooms.

## How to get your cat to use a new bed

- **Put it where your cat already sleeps.** If your cat naps on the couch, put the bed on the couch.
- **Make it smell familiar.** Add a blanket or T-shirt your cat has slept on.
- **Try catnip or silver vine** on the bed, or drop a few treats in it.
- **Be patient.** Some cats ignore a new bed for a few days, then adopt it as their own.

## Safety tips

- **Use only heated beds made for pets.** Human heating pads and electric blankets can get too hot and cause burns, especially in older or less mobile cats. Make sure your cat can always move off a heated bed if it gets too warm.
- **Check cords** on heated beds regularly for chewing, and unplug any bed that's damaged.
- **Wash beds every week or two**, and more often during flea treatment (see our guide to [treating fleas](/guides/how-to-treat-cat-fleas/)).
- **Watch for changes in sleep.** If your cat suddenly sleeps much more, hides away, stops jumping up to its usual spots, or seems restless, see your vet. These can be signs of pain, illness or stress.

## Common questions

### Do cats actually need a bed?

Not strictly, but a warm, safe sleeping spot helps cats feel secure. It also gives older cats a comfortable place to rest their joints and keeps hair off your furniture.

### Why won't my cat sleep in its new bed?

It may be in the wrong place, the wrong shape for how your cat sleeps, or it may just smell new. Move it to where your cat already naps and add a familiar blanket.

### Are heated cat beds safe?

Pet-specific heated beds designed to warm only to around a cat's body temperature are generally safe when used as directed. Avoid human heating pads, and make sure your cat can always get off the bed.

### What's the best bed for a senior cat?

A thick, orthopedic or heated bed with a low entry, placed somewhere warm that doesn't need a jump to reach.

### Should my cat sleep in my bed?

That's a personal choice. Many cats love sleeping with their people. Keep your cat on regular flea and parasite prevention, and give it a bed of its own too, for when it wants its own space.

## The short version

Choose a warm, soft bed that matches how your cat sleeps, usually a donut or bolster bed, and put it somewhere quiet, warm and slightly raised. For seniors, go orthopedic or heated with a low entry.
