Before you treat it as fussiness, ask one question: is this new? A cat that has always been choosy about flavors is a picky eater. A cat that suddenly eats less, or stops, is telling you something is wrong, and that needs a vet rather than a new brand of food. For the truly picky cat, small changes to how food is served often matter more than what's in the bowl.

Cats should never be left to "get hungry enough" to eat. When a cat stops eating, its body breaks down fat faster than the liver can handle, and it can develop a serious condition called hepatic lipidosis (fatty liver). Overweight cats are at the highest risk. It can develop within a few days of not eating.

## Why cats get choosy

- **Smell matters more than taste.** Cats decide largely by aroma. A blocked nose from a cold, or food that's been sitting out, can put them off completely.
- **Texture and shape.** Many cats have firm preferences: pâté or chunks, small kibble or large. Kittens often stick with what they were fed early in life.
- **Too much choice.** Constantly switching foods to tempt a cat can teach it to hold out for something better.
- **The bowl itself.** Deep, narrow bowls that brush the whiskers, or plastic bowls that hold smells, can make eating unpleasant.
- **Where food is served.** Next to the litter box, in a busy hallway, or beside another cat it doesn't get along with.
- **Stress**, from a move, visitors or changes in routine.
- **Pain or illness**, especially dental disease, nausea, kidney disease and many others. This is why a sudden change needs a vet.

## What to try at home

These are for a healthy cat that is choosy but still eating something.

1. **Serve wet food at body temperature.** Warm it slightly (stir well and test it so there are no hot spots) to release the smell. Never serve it straight from the fridge.
2. **Use a shallow, wide dish** made of ceramic, glass or stainless steel, and wash it after every meal. [See shallow cat food dishes](amazon:shallow wide cat food bowl ceramic).
3. **Keep food fresh.** Throw away wet food left out for more than an hour or two, and store open dry food in its bag inside an airtight container.
4. **Pick a quiet spot**, away from the litter box and water bowl, where the cat can see anyone coming. In multi-cat homes, feed cats apart.
5. **Feed set meals**, rather than leaving a full bowl out all day, so your cat comes to meals with an appetite. Small, frequent meals suit cats best.
6. **Test textures, not just flavors.** If your cat refuses one pâté, try a chunks-in-gravy or shredded style from a complete and balanced brand.
7. **Change foods slowly.** Mix the new food in gradually over 7 to 10 days. A sudden swap is more likely to be refused, and can upset the stomach.
8. **Use toppers sparingly.** A teaspoon of a lickable cat treat or a sprinkle of freeze-dried meat on top can tempt a cat back to its meal. Keep extras within 10% of daily calories; our guide to [healthy cat treats](/guides/healthy-cat-treats/) explains why. [See freeze-dried cat food toppers](amazon:freeze dried chicken cat food topper).
9. **Offer food by hand** or on a flat plate, and sit quietly nearby. Some cats eat better with company.
10. **Make mealtimes calm.** If the change began after a move or a new pet, our guide to [helping a stressed cat](/guides/how-to-help-stressed-anxious-cat/) can help.

Give each change a few days, and change one thing at a time so you can tell what worked.

## Things to avoid

- **Waiting it out**, or holding back food to force a cat to eat.
- **Human food as a main meal.** Plain cooked chicken or fish can tempt a cat in the short term, but it isn't a balanced diet on its own.
- **Onion, garlic, chives and leeks** in any form, including stock, broth and baby food that contain onion powder. They damage a cat's red blood cells.
- **Lots of canned tuna for people.** It isn't balanced for cats and can crowd out their regular food.
- **Cow's milk.** Many adult cats can't digest it well and get diarrhea.
- **Giving your cat medicine to boost appetite** without a vet. Appetite stimulants exist, but only your vet can decide if one is right and how to give it.

## When a cat that won't eat needs a vet

Call your vet the same day if your cat:

- Has eaten little or nothing for **24 hours** (sooner for kittens, which need food more often)
- Is overweight and has stopped eating
- Refuses food but still seems interested, then walks away (often a sign of mouth pain or nausea)
- Is vomiting, has diarrhea or seems lethargic or hidden away. See our guide to [cat vomiting](/guides/how-to-treat-cat-vomiting/).
- Is losing weight, drinking or peeing much more than usual, or has bad breath or yellow gums or eyes
- Has stopped eating after starting a new medicine

Your vet will look for the cause, may treat nausea or pain, and can prescribe appetite support or a special diet where needed, given as your vet prescribes.

## Your questions answered

### How long can a cat go without eating?
Far less time than a dog. A cat that hasn't eaten for a day should see a vet, and even a few days without food can lead to fatty liver disease.

### Should I keep changing foods until my cat likes one?
No. Try a few textures from complete and balanced brands, then stick with what works. Constant switching can make a cat fussier and upset its stomach.

### Why does my cat eat treats but not its food?
Treats are designed to be very tasty. Cut back on treats so your cat comes to meals hungry, and keep them under 10% of daily calories. If your cat won't eat proper meals even then, ask your vet.

### Is it OK to warm up my cat's food in the microwave?
Yes, briefly, as long as you stir it well and check that it's only lukewarm, close to body temperature. Never use a metal can or foil pouch in the microwave.
