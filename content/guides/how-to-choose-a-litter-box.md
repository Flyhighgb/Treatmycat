Most litter box problems come down to the box, the litter or the location, not a "naughty" cat. Cats are clean, private animals, and if the setup doesn't suit them, they'll find somewhere else to go. The quick answer: choose a **big, uncovered box** (about one and a half times your cat's length), fill it with **unscented, fine-grained clumping litter**, put it somewhere **quiet but easy to reach**, and have **one more box than you have cats**.

## What to look for in a litter box

**Big enough.** Most commercial boxes are too small. Your cat should be able to turn around, dig and squat without touching the sides. Aim for a box about **1.5 times your cat's length** from nose to the base of the tail. For many cats, a large plastic storage tub with one side cut down works better than anything sold as a litter box.

**Open or covered?** Many people prefer covered boxes, but many cats don't. Covers trap odors inside, which a cat's sensitive nose dislikes, and they can make cats feel cornered. Start with an open box. If your cat kicks litter out or sprays high, try a **high-sided** box with a low entry instead of a lid.

**An easy way in.** Kittens, senior cats and cats with arthritis need a **low entry**, around 3 to 4 inches (8 to 10 cm). Top-entry boxes reduce tracking but aren't suitable for older or stiff cats.

**Easy to clean.** Smooth plastic with no tight corners is easiest to scoop and wash. Avoid liners if your cat's claws catch in them.

## Types of litter

Studies of cat preferences, and feline vets, generally favor **unscented, fine-grained clumping litter**. It feels like sand, which is what cats naturally dig in. Scented litters smell nice to people but can put cats off.

| Clumping clay | Most cats' favorite. Easy to scoop, good odor control. | Heavy, and can be dusty. Choose low-dust and unscented. | [Clumping clay litter](amazon:unscented clumping clay cat litter low dust) |

## Where to put the box

1. **Somewhere quiet**, away from noisy appliances like washing machines, and away from busy doorways.
2. **Away from food and water.** Cats don't like to eat near where they go.
3. **Easy to reach.** In a multi-story home, have a box on every floor, especially for older cats.
4. **Not in a dead end.** A cat should be able to see who's coming and leave easily, particularly in multi-cat homes.
5. **Spread out.** In multi-cat homes, put boxes in different places, not side by side, so one cat can't guard them all.

## Keeping it clean

- **Scoop at least once a day**, and twice if you can.
- Keep litter about **2 inches (5 cm) deep** for most cats. Some cats like it deeper.
- **Empty and wash the whole box** every week or two with hot water and mild unscented soap. Avoid strong-smelling cleaners, bleach and products containing phenols, which are toxic to cats.
- Replace plastic boxes once they become scratched and hold odors.

## When it's not the box

If your cat suddenly stops using the litter box, **see your vet first**. Peeing or pooping outside the box is often a sign of a medical problem such as a urinary tract problem, constipation, arthritis or kidney disease. **A cat straining in the box with little or no urine is an emergency**, especially a male cat (see our guide to [urinary tract problems in cats](/guides/how-to-help-cat-urinary-tract-problem/)). Changes in the size or number of clumps, or in the stool, are also worth mentioning to your vet (see our guide to [constipation](/guides/how-to-treat-cat-constipation/)).

## Common questions

### How many litter boxes do I need?

The usual rule is one per cat plus one extra, so two boxes for one cat and three for two. Put them in different places around your home.

### Should I get a covered or uncovered litter box?

Start with uncovered. Most cats prefer open boxes, which smell fresher to them and feel safer. If your cat kicks litter everywhere, try a high-sided open box before adding a lid.

### What's the best litter for cats?

Most cats prefer unscented, fine-grained clumping litter. Beyond that, it depends on your cat. If you want to try a different type, offer it in a second box so your cat can choose.

### Why does my cat kick litter out of the box?

Usually the box is too small or the sides too low. A larger, high-sided box with a low entry, and a litter mat outside, usually helps.

## The short version

Choose a big, open box with a low entry, fill it with unscented clumping litter, and have one more box than you have cats, in quiet, easy-to-reach places. Scoop daily. If your cat suddenly stops using the box, see your vet first.
