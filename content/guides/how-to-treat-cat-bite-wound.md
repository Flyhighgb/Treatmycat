If your cat goes outside, sooner or later it may come home from a fight. Cat bites look tiny, often just a small puncture or a bit of matted fur, but they're deceptive. A cat's sharp teeth push bacteria deep under the skin, and the skin seals over quickly, trapping infection inside. A few days later, that small bite can turn into a painful, swollen **abscess**. That's why most cat bite wounds need a vet, even when they look minor.

## Signs your cat has a bite or scratch wound

Bites are often hard to find under fur. Look for:

- **Small puncture wounds**, scabs or a patch of wet or matted fur, often on the face, legs, base of the tail or back
- **Swelling** that feels warm, soft or firm
- **Pain** when touched, or your cat flinching or growling when you stroke one spot
- **Limping**, if a leg or paw was bitten
- **Pus** or a bad smell, sometimes a sudden burst of discharge when an abscess ruptures
- **Lethargy, hiding or not eating**, which often means fever from infection

An abscess usually appears **2 to 5 days after the fight**, so the first sign may be a quiet cat with a lump, rather than an obvious wound.

## Get emergency help straight away if

- The wound is **bleeding heavily** and doesn't stop after 5 minutes of firm pressure with a clean cloth
- The wound is **deep or gaping**, or you can see muscle or bone
- It's on the **chest or belly**, near the **eye**, or your cat has **trouble breathing**
- Your cat was **bitten by a dog** or a **wild animal**. Crush injuries under the skin can be much worse than they look, and wild animals can carry rabies.
- Your cat is **collapsing**, very weak or in severe pain
- Your cat was **hit by a car** or fell from a height, even if it seems fine

## First aid for a minor wound

These steps are for small scratches or bites while you arrange a vet visit. They don't replace one.

1. **Stay calm and keep yourself safe.** Hurt cats can bite. Wrap your cat loosely in a towel if needed.
2. **Stop any bleeding** by pressing gently on the wound with a clean cloth or gauze for a few minutes.
3. **Clean gently.** Rinse the area with clean, lukewarm water or sterile saline. You can trim fur around the wound with blunt-ended scissors if it's easy and safe to do so, but don't risk cutting the skin.
4. **Don't cover it tightly.** Bandages can trap infection and are easy to put on too tightly.
5. **Stop licking.** If your cat keeps licking or scratching the wound, a soft recovery collar helps ([see soft recovery collars](amazon:soft recovery collar for cats)).
6. **Keep your cat indoors** and quiet, and check the wound twice a day for swelling, heat or discharge.
7. **Call your vet.** Most bite wounds need antibiotics, and starting them early can prevent an abscess.

## How vets treat bites and abscesses

Your vet will examine the wound and check your cat's temperature. For a fresh bite, they may clean it and prescribe antibiotics. For an abscess, they will usually drain it, flush it out, and may leave it open or place a drain so it can heal from the inside. Your cat may need pain relief and, sometimes, sedation for this. Give all medicine exactly as prescribed and finish the course, even if your cat seems better.

If an abscess has already burst at home, gently clean away the discharge with warm water and a soft cloth, wear gloves, and still take your cat to the vet.

## Never

- **Use hydrogen peroxide or rubbing alcohol** on the wound. They damage healthy tissue and slow healing.
- **Use tea tree oil or other essential oils.** They're toxic to cats.
- **Apply human creams or ointments** unless your vet says so. Cats lick them off, and some ingredients, such as zinc oxide, are harmful if swallowed.
- **Squeeze or try to lance a lump** yourself. You can push infection deeper and hurt your cat.
- **Give human painkillers.** Acetaminophen (Tylenol) is deadly to cats, and ibuprofen and aspirin can cause serious harm.
- **Use leftover antibiotics** from another pet or an old prescription.

## Infections that spread through bites

Cat fights can spread serious viral infections, including **feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV)** and **feline leukemia virus (FeLV)**. Your vet may suggest testing a few weeks after a fight, especially if your cat isn't vaccinated against FeLV. Make sure your cat's **rabies** vaccine is up to date, as many US states require. If your cat was bitten by a wild animal or an unknown animal, tell your vet straight away.

## Preventing fight wounds

- **Spay or neuter your cat.** Unneutered males fight far more and roam farther.
- **Keep your cat in at night**, when most fights happen, or consider keeping it indoors or using a secure catio.
- **Keep vaccinations up to date**, including rabies and, for outdoor cats, FeLV.
- **Check your cat over** when it comes home, especially if it's been gone longer than usual or seems out of sorts.

## Questions cat owners ask

### Will a cat bite heal on its own?

Small scratches often do. But bite punctures frequently become infected and turn into abscesses, so it's best to have any bite checked by a vet, ideally within 24 hours.

### How do I know if my cat's wound is infected?

Look for swelling, warmth, redness, pus, a bad smell, or a cat that is quiet, hiding or not eating. Infected wounds need a vet.

### What does a cat abscess look like?

A soft or firm swelling under the skin, often warm and painful, that may burst and leak smelly pus. Your cat may have a fever and seem unwell.

### Can I clean my cat's wound with saline?

Yes. Sterile saline or clean, lukewarm water is the safest way to rinse a minor wound at home. Avoid hydrogen peroxide and alcohol.

### Do I need to worry if my cat bit me?

Cat bites to people often get infected too. Wash the bite well with soap and water and contact your doctor, especially if it's a puncture wound or on the hand.

## The short version

Cat bites look small but often turn into painful abscesses a few days later. Rinse minor wounds with clean water or saline, stop your cat licking, and see your vet for antibiotics. Deep wounds, heavy bleeding, dog or wild animal bites, or a cat that seems unwell need a vet straight away.
