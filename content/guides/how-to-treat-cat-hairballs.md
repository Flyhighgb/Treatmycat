Cats swallow loose hair while grooming. Most of it passes through the gut, but some clumps in the stomach and comes back up as a hairball. An occasional hairball is normal, especially in long-haired cats. **Frequent hairballs (more than once or twice a month) are not normal** and often point to over-grooming, a gut problem or a skin problem.

## What a hairball looks like

A hairball is usually a damp, sausage-shaped clump of fur, often brought up after a few retching or hacking sounds. It's usually long and thin rather than round, because it's shaped by the esophagus on the way up. It may come up with a little clear fluid or food.

## Why some cats get more hairballs

- **Long coats.** Persians, Maine Coons, Ragdolls and other long-haired breeds swallow more hair.
- **Shedding seasons.** Hairballs often increase in spring and fall.
- **Over-grooming.** Itchy skin from [fleas](/guides/how-to-treat-cat-fleas/) or allergies, pain or stress can make a cat groom far more than usual.
- **Slow gut movement.** Some cats, especially older ones or those with bowel problems, don't move hair through the gut as easily.

## How to reduce hairballs

**Brush your cat regularly.** This is the single most effective thing you can do. Every hair in the brush is a hair your cat doesn't swallow.

- Short-haired cats: 2 to 3 times a week
- Long-haired cats: daily, with a wide-toothed comb for tangles
- Brush more often during spring and fall shedding

Short, gentle sessions with a treat at the end work better than one long battle. A rubber grooming mitt is a good start for cats that dislike brushes.

**Try a hairball-control food.** These contain extra fiber that helps hair move through the gut. Switch gradually over 7 to 10 days to avoid an upset stomach.

**Increase water intake.** Wet food, a water fountain and several water bowls around the home all help keep things moving.

**Hairball gels or pastes.** Lubricant gels (often malt-flavored) can help hair pass. Use them as the label directs, and not as a substitute for brushing. Don't give them at the same time as medicines, as they can reduce absorption.

**Keep your cat busy.** Boredom and stress can lead to over-grooming. Daily play sessions, climbing spaces and window perches help.

## Things not to do

- Don't give butter, cooking oil or mineral oil by mouth. Oils can be inhaled into the lungs and cause pneumonia.
- Don't give human laxatives.
- Don't shave a long-haired cat at home to stop hairballs. Cat skin is thin and cuts easily. A groomer or vet can do a safe trim if needed.

## Is it really a hairball?

Hairballs are often blamed for other problems:

- **Coughing with no hairball coming up**, especially with the neck stretched out low to the ground, can be **feline asthma**. Film it on your phone and show your vet.
- **Vomiting food or liquid** regularly, with or without hair, can be a sign of food sensitivity, inflammatory bowel disease, kidney or thyroid disease, or other conditions.

## See a vet right away if your cat

- Retches repeatedly without bringing anything up
- Stops eating, or is [constipated](/guides/how-to-treat-cat-constipation/) or not passing stools
- Has a swollen or painful belly
- Is lethargic or hiding

These can be signs of a blockage, which is an emergency.

## Common questions

### How often is it normal for a cat to have hairballs?

For most cats, an occasional hairball, no more than once or twice a month, is normal. More often than that is worth a vet check, as it can point to over-grooming or a gut problem.

### Do hairball treats work?

Hairball treats and foods usually rely on added fiber, which can help hair pass. They are best used alongside regular brushing, not instead of it.

### Can hairballs cause a blockage?

Rarely, yes. A large hairball can get stuck in the stomach or intestines. Repeated unproductive retching, not eating, constipation or a painful belly are warning signs that need a vet the same day.

### Why does my cat cough but no hairball comes up?

A cat that hacks or coughs regularly without bringing up a hairball may have asthma or another airway problem rather than hairballs. Record a video of the episode and show your vet.

### Should I worry if my cat vomits a hairball right after eating?

Vomiting soon after a meal is more often from eating too fast than from hair. Try smaller meals, a slow feeder or a puzzle feeder. If your cat vomits food regularly, with or without hair, see your vet, since it can be a sign of food sensitivity or bowel disease.

## The short version

Brush often, add fiber and water, and keep an eye on how often it happens. More than once or twice a month is worth a vet check.
