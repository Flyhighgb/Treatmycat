Cats hide pain and illness, so by the time something is obvious it can already be serious. Use this list to decide how fast to act, and **print it** (or save it as a PDF) so it's there when you need it. <button class="button print-button" onclick="window.print()">Print this checklist</button>

## Go to a vet or emergency clinic now

Call ahead on the way if you can. Don't wait to see if it passes.

- [ ] Straining in the litter box with little or no urine, crying while trying to pee, or a lot of licking at the genitals. A **blocked bladder** can be fatal within a day or two, especially in male cats.
- [ ] Breathing with the mouth open, panting, fast or noisy breathing, or blue, gray or very pale gums.
- [ ] Collapse, sudden weakness, can't stand or walk, or suddenly dragging the back legs.
- [ ] A seizure, or a cat that is very hard to wake.
- [ ] Ate or licked something toxic: lilies (any part, even pollen or vase water), human medicines such as Tylenol or ibuprofen, antifreeze, or a dog flea product.
- [ ] Hit by a car, a fall from a height, or another major injury, even if your cat seems fine afterward.
- [ ] Heavy bleeding that doesn't stop with gentle pressure after a few minutes.
- [ ] Repeated vomiting with no food staying down, or vomiting blood.
- [ ] A swollen, hard or painful belly.
- [ ] Very high or low body temperature: hot to the touch and panting, or cold and limp.
- [ ] Thread, string, tinsel or ribbon hanging from the mouth or bottom. **Don't pull it.**

## Call your vet today

Same-day advice is wise for these.

- [ ] Hasn't eaten anything for 24 hours. Cats that stop eating can develop serious liver problems, so don't wait it out.
- [ ] Diarrhea or vomiting with lethargy, or that lasts more than a day.
- [ ] A kitten, senior or a cat with a known illness that seems unwell in any way.
- [ ] An eye that is suddenly cloudy, held shut, or looks injured.
- [ ] A bite wound or a swelling that's hot and painful. Bite wounds often turn into abscesses.
- [ ] Limping that doesn't improve within a few hours, or won't put any weight on a leg.
- [ ] Head tilting, loss of balance or walking in circles.
- [ ] Drinking or peeing much more than usual, all of a sudden.

## Book a vet visit soon

These aren't emergencies, but they need checking within a few days.

- [ ] Gradual weight loss, or a change in appetite that lasts more than a few days.
- [ ] Bad breath, drooling, or eating on one side of the mouth.
- [ ] Peeing or pooping outside the litter box.
- [ ] Scratching, hair loss or scabs that don't settle.
- [ ] Hairballs or vomiting more than about once or twice a month.
- [ ] Hiding more, sleeping more, or no longer jumping up to favorite spots.
- [ ] A new lump that's still there after a week, or any lump that's growing.
